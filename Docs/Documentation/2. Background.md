# 2. Background

## 2.1 Problem Statement

### 2.1.1 General Problem

The Medical-Dental Services (MDS) of the Technological Institute of the Philippines (TIP) relies on a fully manual, paper-based system for managing the medical and dental records of its student and employee population across multiple campus locations (Manila — Arlegui and Casal campuses — and Quezon City). This dependency on physical logbooks, handwritten forms, and manual filing systems results in significant operational inefficiencies, data fragmentation, limited accessibility, and an inability to generate timely analytics required for institutional health reporting.

### 2.1.2 Specific Problems Identified

* **Problem 1: Fragmented and Inaccessible Records.** Patient medical histories, dental records, consultation notes, prescriptions, and laboratory results are maintained in physical folders distributed across campus clinic locations. Retrieving a single student's complete health profile requires manual searching through multiple filing cabinets, consuming valuable clinic staff time and delaying patient care. Cross-campus record sharing is virtually non-existent.

* **Problem 2: Unstructured Appointment and Consultation Workflow.** Appointment scheduling is conducted manually, with no real-time visibility into slot availability, no automated acceptance or rejection of booking requests, and no mechanism for patients to track appointment status. Clinic staff cannot dynamically adjust appointment quotas (e.g., 60 medical slots and 1 dental slot per session) without rewriting physical schedules.

* **Problem 3: Absence of Standardized Diagnosis Coding and Reporting.** Medical consultations are documented without standardized ICD-10 coding, making it difficult to classify diagnoses, generate morbidity reports, or track disease patterns across semesters. Annual reporting to the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) requires labor-intensive manual tabulation of health statistics.

* **Problem 4: Insecure and Non-Compliant Data Handling.** Paper records are vulnerable to unauthorized physical access, loss, damage, and lack the encryption, audit logging, and access control mechanisms required for compliance with the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (R.A. 10173). There is no verifiable chain of custody for patient data modifications and no systematic enforcement of role-based access restrictions among clinic personnel.

* **Problem 5: No Digital Communication Channel.** Students seeking medical consultations must visit the clinic in person, with no option for virtual consultations. There is no mechanism for real-time patient-to-staff communication, automated notification delivery, or document sharing — forcing patients to make multiple clinic visits for follow-ups, prescription pickups, and certificate collection.

* **Problem 6: Manual Inventory Management.** Tracking of medical supplies, medicines, and equipment is done through physical stock cards, with no automated low-stock alerts, no batch-level expiry tracking, no digital dispensing records, and no way to reconcile inventory across multiple clinic locations in real time.

### 2.1.3 Impact of the Problem

* **Operational Impact:** Clinic staff spend a significant portion of their working hours on administrative tasks — locating records, manually scheduling appointments, hand-computing inventory levels, and tabulating statistics — rather than providing direct patient care. The absence of a centralized digital system creates workflow bottlenecks that reduce overall clinic throughput.

* **Patient Impact:** Students and employees experience long wait times, repeated in-person visits for routine transactions (appointment booking, certificate collection, prescription re-issuance), and a lack of transparency regarding their appointment and record status. The absence of a virtual consultation option is particularly consequential for students unable to physically visit the clinic during operating hours.

* **Compliance Impact:** Without encrypted digital storage, role-based access control, and audit trails, the institution faces the risk of non-compliance with R.A. 10173 and is exposed to potential data breaches affecting sensitive personal health information.

* **Institutional Impact:** The inability to generate automated, standardized health analytics limits the institution's capacity to identify public health trends within its population, make data-driven resource allocation decisions, and fulfill regulatory reporting obligations to DOLE, CHED, and DOH.

## 2.2 Proposed Solution

### 2.2.1 Proposed System

The MDSystem is a full-stack, multi-platform Electronic Medical Record (EMR) system comprising a Node.js/Express 5 backend API (serving both REST and GraphQL endpoints), two React 19 web portals (patient-facing and staff-facing), a React Native/Expo mobile application, and a shared core business logic package — all backed by PostgreSQL, Redis, and BullMQ.

### 2.2.2 System Architecture Overview

The system employs a **dual-server architecture**: a Patient API server (port 3000) serving the patient web portal and mobile app, and a Staff API server (port 3001) serving the staff portal. Both servers share the same Express 5 codebase, configuration, middleware, database connection pool, and utility libraries, but mount different subsets of routes with role-appropriate JWT enforcement. Socket.IO with a Redis adapter provides real-time event delivery across both server instances, while BullMQ manages asynchronous email job processing.

### 2.2.3 Expected Benefits

* **Digitized Health Records:** Complete longitudinal patient histories stored in a relational database with over 60 tables, eliminating paper dependency and enabling instant retrieval of any patient's medical, dental, allergy, immunization, and lifestyle data.

* **Streamlined Clinical Workflows:** Structured status-driven pipelines for record submission and approval (InProgress → Pending → Approved/Revision/Rejected), appointment scheduling (Pending → Scheduled → Completed/NoShow), consultation management (Created → Open → Completed/Referred/Monitored), and medicine dispensing — reducing administrative overhead and standardizing clinical processes.

* **Real-Time Communication:** Socket.IO-powered health chat with typing indicators, file sharing, and Expo push notifications enabling virtual patient-staff consultations without requiring in-person visits.

* **Automated Reporting and Analytics:** 30+ built-in analytics queries with multi-format export, eliminating manual tabulation and enabling real-time institutional health surveillance.

* **Regulatory Compliance:** JWT-based authentication with 2FA, AES-256 encrypted TOTP secrets, role-based access control with 40+ granular permissions, IP rate limiting, XSS protection, magic-byte file validation, and complete audit trails — ensuring compliance with the Data Privacy Act and institutional policies.

* **Multi-Campus Support:** Branch-scoped data access (Manila, Quezon City, or Both), location-aware inventory management, and campus-specific appointment scheduling enabling a unified system across all TIP campus clinics.

## 2.3 Theoretical Concepts and Related Work

### 2.3.1 Electronic Medical Records (EMR) in Healthcare

Electronic Medical Records represent the digital transformation of traditional paper-based health records. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), EMR systems are essential for improving healthcare quality, patient safety, and operational efficiency in clinical settings. The adoption of EMR systems in educational institutions addresses the unique challenge of managing transient student populations, where records must be organized by academic period (semester), and data retention policies must account for graduation-triggered archival.

### 2.3.2 ICD-10/ICD-11 Diagnostic Coding

The International Classification of Diseases, developed by the WHO, provides a standardized alphanumeric coding system for classifying diagnoses. MDSystem integrates the WHO ICD-11 API with a local PostgreSQL caching layer (`ICDLookup` table), enabling clinic doctors to assign standardized diagnosis codes during consultations. This integration supports eight diagnosis type classifications (Primary, Secondary, Differential, RuledOut, Provisional, Complication, Chronic, FollowUp) and enables automated morbidity reporting.

### 2.3.3 Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) and the Principle of Least Privilege

The RBAC model implemented in MDSystem aligns with NIST SP 800-53 access control guidelines. The system defines 40+ granular permission keys organized across 11 functional modules (EMR, Profile, Appointments, Consultations, Inventory, Health Chat, Analytics, Notifications, Role Management, Announcements, Dashboard). Permissions are assigned per staff member with branch scoping (Manila, Quezon City, or Both), and permission templates enable rapid role provisioning for common staff roles (Doctor, Dentist, Nurse, Admin).

### 2.3.4 Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (R.A. 10173)

R.A. 10173 mandates that personal information controllers implement reasonable and appropriate organizational, physical, and technical measures to protect personal data — particularly sensitive personal information such as health records — against unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, and destruction. MDSystem addresses these requirements through encryption at rest (AES-256 for TOTP secrets, bcrypt for passwords) and in transit (HTTPS, Helmet CSP headers), consent management (versioned data privacy agreements rendered during registration and login), and audit logging.

### 2.3.5 Real-Time Communication Architectures

The system's real-time infrastructure is built on the WebSocket protocol via Socket.IO, augmented with a Redis pub/sub adapter for horizontal scalability across the dual-server architecture. This pattern follows the established microservice event bus design, where a centralized message broker (Redis) ensures that events emitted on one server instance are propagated to clients connected to the other. The offline notification queue (implemented as Redis lists with atomic MULTI/EXEC flush operations) addresses the mobile-first challenge of unreliable network connectivity.

### 2.3.6 Factory-Based Dependency Injection for Cross-Platform Code Sharing

The `@mdsystem/core` shared package employs a factory pattern — `createTokenService()`, `createAxiosRequestHandler()`, `createSocketService()` — that accepts platform-specific adapters as constructor dependencies. This approach, inspired by the Dependency Inversion Principle (SOLID), enables identical business logic to execute across web browsers (using `localStorage` and `window.location`) and React Native (using `AsyncStorage` and navigation refs) without conditional platform checks in the shared code.

## 2.4 Scope and Limitations

### 2.4.1 Scope

* The system covers the complete lifecycle of student and employee medical-dental records within TIP's Medical-Dental Services.
* Supported user roles: Patient (Student, Employee), Medical Staff (Doctor, Dentist, Nurse), and Administrator.
* The system operates across three campus locations: Arlegui (Manila), Casal (Manila), and Quezon City.
* Supported platforms: Desktop browsers (staff portal), Desktop and mobile browsers (patient portal), and native mobile app (Android and iOS via Expo).
* The system integrates with the WHO ICD-11 API for diagnostic coding and supports external file uploads with magic-byte validation.

### 2.4.2 Limitations

* The system does not integrate with the TIP Student Information System (SIS) database in the current version; student and employee registration is handled within MDSystem through institutional email validation (`@tip.edu.ph` domain enforcement).
* Payment processing and insurance claim management are outside the scope of this system.
* The AI-powered e-consultation chatbot is an external microservice; its availability depends on a separate FastAPI deployment.
* The mobile application does not support offline mode — a stable internet connection is required for all operations.
* SMS notifications are not implemented; the system relies on email (via SMTP) and push notifications (via Expo).
* The system is designed for the medical and dental domains and does not extend to psychological counseling or other specialized health services.
